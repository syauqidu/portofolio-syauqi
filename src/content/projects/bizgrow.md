## Project overview

BizGrow is an academic web application project designed to help micro, small, and medium enterprises (UMKM) understand their business through sales and inventory data. The concept connects transaction recording, stock monitoring, and forecasting to support business planning.

I contributed as Project Manager, Backend Developer, and QA, with additional responsibilities in system analysis and technical documentation. The project was presented at an academic exhibition, while development remained incomplete and the application was not publicly deployed.

## Problem statement

The project addressed the difficulties small businesses can face in sustaining growth after reaching a certain scale. BizGrow's focus was on using sales and inventory information to help owners plan product needs and decide when to replenish stock.

The intended benefit was more informed purchasing, with better visibility into potential overstocking or shortages. This was the motivation for the product, rather than a verified business outcome.

## My role and contributions

### Project management

I participated in managing and coordinating the team's work throughout development. This responsibility connected my technical contributions with the wider progress of the academic project.

### System analysis and documentation

I contributed to requirements analysis and system design, and prepared the **Software Requirements Specification (SRS)** and **Software Design Document (SDD)** to support implementation.

### Backend development

I contributed to the application backend using **Laravel 11**. The development scope included authentication and authorization, Admin and UMKM roles, account verification, product and transaction management, sales and stock history, profit information, and REST API endpoints.

These areas describe the backend work and discussions within the project. They do not represent a list of features I independently completed or a fully implemented system.

### Quality assurance

I participated in software testing to check functionality and evaluate whether the implementation matched the system requirements.

## Development lifecycle

The project provided experience across four connected development activities:

1. **Requirements analysis:** understand the business problem and translate it into system requirements.
2. **System design:** develop the system design and supporting SRS and SDD documentation.
3. **Implementation:** develop the web application and backend using Laravel, REST APIs, and a relational database.
4. **Software testing:** evaluate developed functionality against the defined requirements.

My responsibilities spanned these activities through analysis, documentation, coordination, backend contributions, and testing.

## Core product features

The following areas describe BizGrow's intended product scope. Implementation was incomplete, so they should be read as a combination of development work and feature concepts.

### Product demand forecasting

BizGrow was designed to use historical sales records, including daily transactions, as the basis for estimating demand in a future period. The aim was to help business owners anticipate product needs when planning purchases.

### Inventory planning and buffer stock

The inventory planning concept focused on when stock should be replenished and how buffer stock could be considered alongside product needs and business conditions.

### Sales and inventory management

The supporting scope covered manual sales entry, transaction history, and sales record filtering. Product data management, stock history, and inventory monitoring provided the related inventory context.

### User and UMKM management

The account management scope included authentication, user profiles, Admin and UMKM roles, and UMKM account verification workflows.

### Business insights

Profit information and revenue forecasting were also discussed within the project. Revenue forecasting remained part of the discussed scope and is not presented here as a completed capability.

## Technical implementation

The backend work used **PHP and Laravel 11**, with **MySQL** as the relational database. **REST API** endpoints formed part of the backend scope, and **Laravel Sanctum** was used for authentication work.

The SRS and SDD documented requirements and system design to support development. This case study focuses on the confirmed stack and my backend involvement; it does not attribute a specific forecasting algorithm or prediction accuracy to the application.

## Testing and evaluation

I participated in testing the developed functionality to evaluate its behavior and alignment with requirements. The testing contribution was part of the broader software engineering process alongside analysis, design, and implementation.

The application still had unfinished features and implementation work when it was presented. Testing participation should therefore be understood within that development context.

## Project exhibition

Our team showcased BizGrow at an academic project exhibition as part of the Proyek Tingkat assessment. We demonstrated the application's progress and feature concepts to visitors or assessors.

The exhibition presented the team's work at that stage of development.

## Outcome and limitations

BizGrow provided hands-on experience across requirements analysis, system design, team coordination, backend development, and software testing. My contribution combined technical documentation and implementation with project management and QA responsibilities.

The system remained incomplete, with some features and implementation work unfinished. It was not publicly deployed or production-ready. The project outcome was an academic development effort and exhibition demonstration, without verified forecasting performance or measured business impact.
